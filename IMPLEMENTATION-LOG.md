## Installer and updater

Building decision 54 (`docs/installation-and-updates.md`) on every desktop, 23 October 2026, while the owner was away, as the owner instructed: "do everything, do not stop, make the decisions you need". The decisions are 68 to 77 in `docs/architecture.md` section 2, in a block of their own, their numbers provisional until this work is merged.

| 13 | The strip's offer: a button in the strip? | A notice, once ("NeoTeletext 0.3 is available: Install it in Settings"); "Install" is in the settings panel with the version and the trusted core's line about updates (decision 76). | The strip's address field stays the channel's; the panel is where settings act. |
| 14 | Does the updater use the user's proxy? | Yes, and its sandbox's one port is then the proxy's, which the broker reads from the updater's arguments with the engine's parser (decision 76). | Requirement 19.10's rule: nothing goes direct when a proxy is set. |

| 16 | Windows packaging: `ALL APPLICATION PACKAGES`, the containers' profiles, a fetcher's loopback exemption, the interface's container. | The installer grants read and execute on `versions\` with no administrator; the profiles are made at first start and removed at uninstall; the loopback exemption needs an administrator and is not given; the interface keeps its restricted token (decision 73). | What needs an administrator is said, never done by a per-user installer. |
| 17 | The uninstaller cannot remove itself while running on Windows. | A copy in `%TEMP%` does the removal once the launcher has ended, and stays there (decision 77). | `MoveFileEx`'s delayed delete needs an administrator. |

### Status, 23 October 2026

**Done and verified.** The launcher, the stub, the Windows installer, the confined updater, the release tool and the browser's side of updates, on macOS, Linux and Windows (decisions 68 to 77). Each platform's tests pass (launcher, updater and release tool, the updater's probe on Linux), the fuzz targets of every new reader ran clean, and each platform's end-to-end test passed: install, a healthy start, an update staged by the confined updater and committed, a crashing release rolled back, a release not signed by the pinned signer refused, removal; on macOS and Linux also expired metadata and a full disk, and on Linux a launcher release. The AppArmor profile was proven on the Linux host with the restriction on. The details are `docs/installation-and-updates.md` section 15.
